## Streaming pace, part 1

The kinetic energy of a body is $T=\tfrac12 mv^2$, and the work done by a constant force along a displacement is $W=\vec F\cdot\vec d$. Together they give the work-energy theorem, which says that the net work equals the change in kinetic energy.

$$W_{net} = \Delta T = \tfrac12 m v_f^2 - \tfrac12 m v_i^2$$

For a spring with constant $k$ the restoring force is $F=-kx$ and the stored energy is $U=\tfrac12 k x^2$. The angular frequency of the resulting oscillation is $\omega=\sqrt{k/m}$, so the period is $T=2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$.

$$x(t) = A\cos(\omega t + \phi)$$

In a gravitational field the potential energy near the surface is $U=mgh$, and conservation of mechanical energy lets us write the speed at the bottom of a drop of height $h$ as $v=\sqrt{2gh}$. The same reasoning applied to a pendulum gives $T\approx 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$ for small angles.

$$\oint \vec E\cdot d\vec A = \frac{Q_{enc}}{\varepsilon_0}$$

These relations are enough to solve most introductory mechanics problems, and the formulas above should reveal at the same pace as the surrounding words.
